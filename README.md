## Data Analytics within the NBA

The following repository contains datasets, scripts and resulting visualizations which were obtained while writing my Bachelor Thesis at Bocconi University, Milan.

### Purpose

In the last two decades, and even more so in recent years, teams within the National Basketball Association are starting to rely more and more on the use of data to derive actionable insights, with the purpose of ultimately achieving an advantage over opposing teams. 

However, the process which led to the advent of analytics in the NBA is not appreciated by all; indeed, particularly former players argue that the reliance of teams on the use of data has actually damaged the sport itself, as data cannot account for all possible actions that are taking place on the court. While this may be the case, it is also fundamental to clarify that several current players have actually embraced the use of analytics. In fact, players are constantly searching for ways to improve their games, primarily as a method to prolong their careers and remain relevant. 

Thus, while the general sentiment towards analytics in sports is mixed, their use cannot be discredited. This is particulalry evident when taking in consideration the most recent, and agruably radical, change in the way basketball is played: the use of the three-point shot.

### Content

The following Final Work starts by looking at some significant changes that shaped the way current basketball is played. The first of these trends is the increase in pace (i.e: the average time it takes for a team to conclude one offensive possession) that took place over the last two decades, as well as some possible reasons and consequences of this change. 

Then, another major trend is taken into consideration: the significant increase in the use of the three-point shot, which started being more evident in the last decade or so.

Eventually, building on the previous analysis of historical trends, an effort is made at developing a clustering model with k-means technique. The purpose of this clustering is that of grouping players according to their traditional and advanced statistics, rather than using their traditional positions on the floor. This leads to five new groups of players, each with a specific sets of skills, characteristics and on-court production.

These groups are analyzed to assess common trends among players belonging to the same cluster. 

### Further Application

Despite limiting my Final Work to the clustering technique used to group players, a notable further application could be combining our clusters with players' salary data. This would allow to identify the average salary for each cluster, in order to subsequently assess whether a specific player belonging to a cluster is over-paid or under-paid relative to the other members of his cluster, taking into consideration their on-court statistics.
